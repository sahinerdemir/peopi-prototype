WHAT IS PEOPI? · PRODUCT MANAGEMENT CASE STUDY

Product Overview.

Proximity-based professional networking designed to turn physical co-presence into intentional, high-trust relationships.

Peopi is a mobile product concept designed to turn physical proximity into intentional, high-trust professional relationships. In places where relevant peers share the same room—conferences, coworking hubs, and innovation districts—Peopi replaces awkward cold outreach with a permissioned, consent-first introduction loop.

PRODUCT STRATEGY

Strategic choices grounded in human trust.

Strategic differentiators explained through deliberate product reasoning rather than arbitrary feature checklists.

01

Default Invisible Presence

Strategic Choice

Launching or opening the app never broadcasts availability. Presence remains completely dark until an active session is explicitly initiated.

Product Rationale (Why)

Passive beaconing creates pervasive surveillance anxiety. Making visibility an explicit, active choice lowers adoption friction and protects member trust.

02

Time-Limited Discovery Sessions (30, 60, or 120 minutes)

Strategic Choice

Every discovery session runs on an active countdown timer of 30, 60, or 120 minutes. When the session expires, visibility automatically reverts to dark presence.

Product Rationale (Why)

Professional availability is inherently temporary. Time-boxing prevents "zombie profiles" and ensures that anyone visible is genuinely present and available right now.

03

Approximate Proximity & Privacy Safeguards

Strategic Choice

Relative distance rings (Immediate, Nearby, Walking distance) without live GPS coordinates, continuous tracking, or pinpoint maps.

Product Rationale (Why)

Precise coordinates expose members to unwanted tracking. The design aims to reduce physical tracking, triangulation, and inference risks through approximate presence, aggregation thresholds, and consent-first controls.

04

Mutual Acceptance Before Messaging

Strategic Choice

Direct chat unlocks exclusively after an introductory request with a short note is accepted by the recipient.

Product Rationale (Why)

Open inboxes degrade into unsolicited outreach and sales pitches. A double opt-in handshake protects attention, reduces unsolicited messaging, and ensures both parties enter conversation with mutual intent.

05

Miami-First Pilot Density Hypothesis

Strategic Choice

Proposed initial testing strictly within selected Miami coworking spaces (e.g. Brickell, Wynwood) and scheduled tech gatherings.

Product Rationale (Why)

Proximity network effects fail when liquidity is spread too thin. Concentrating hypothetical pilot testing in dense, tech-forward gathering spaces allows evaluating core loop viability before considering expansion.

MVP SCOPE & TRADE-OFFS

Consequential MVP decisions and their trade-offs.

Every product decision requires deliberate trade-offs. Here is how four critical architectural compromises were evaluated for the initial scope.

DECISION 01 Visibility Model

Time-Limited Sessions vs. Always-On Tracking

Selected MVP Path

Explicit 30, 60, or 120-minute session countdown with automatic expiry back to dark presence.

Accepted Trade-Off

Lower recorded background daily active time; in exchange for intentional user privacy, minimal background battery consumption by avoiding continuous tracking, and confirmed live presence.

DECISION 02 Communication Architecture

Consent-Based Handshake vs. Open Direct Messaging

Selected MVP Path

Mandatory introduction request with optional 200-character note; recipient holds total agency to accept or decline.

Accepted Trade-Off

Slower communication velocity and added interaction friction; in exchange for reducing unsolicited messaging, protecting recipient attention, and encouraging intentional introductions.

DECISION 03 Proximity Display

Closed Proximity Rings vs. Exact Pinpoint Maps

Selected MVP Path

Abstract radar preview with coarse distance zones; no coordinates, pin markers, or continuous background GPS.

Accepted Trade-Off

Users cannot pinpoint a person's exact physical desk or chair; in exchange for reducing triangulation, stalking, and location-inference risks through approximate distance zones and aggregation safeguards.

DECISION 04 Audience Positioning

Startup & Tech Focus vs. Broad Professional Categories

Selected MVP Path

Tailored profile tags, headlines, and intent options optimized specifically for founders, builders, and operators.

Accepted Trade-Off

Constrained initial addressable market (TAM); in exchange for high contextual alignment, shared professional vocabulary, and clearer initial focus.

The complete MVP boundary, non-negotiable invariant rules, and trade-off catalog are preserved in Chapter 01.5.

Problem & Opportunity.

Physical proximity creates opportunities for connection, but it does not reveal professional relevance or willingness to engage.

In cafés, coworking hubs, and industry venues, professionals frequently share the same physical space without knowing who nearby shares relevant interests, who is open to meeting right now, or whether an approach would be welcome. This chapter frames that real-world visibility gap as a product opportunity and a testable pilot hypothesis rather than an empirically validated market finding.

01 — THE PROBLEM

Physical proximity, compatibility, and real-time intent are disconnected.

Sharing a physical room solves distance, but it leaves professional relevance and social permission completely invisible.

“Physical proximity creates opportunities for connection, but it does not reveal professional relevance or willingness to engage.”

The underlying problem is not a shortage of capable professionals in physical venues. Rather, three distinct conditions must align for a worthwhile in-person introduction—physical proximity, professional compatibility, and real-time intent—yet only the first is visible to the naked eye. Without a way to bridge the other two, initiating a conversation requires interrupting a stranger with no context.

Four Unanswered Questions When Sharing a Physical Environment

01 · RELEVANCE

Who nearby shares relevant skills or interests?

Everyone working behind a laptop looks identical. Domain expertise, active projects, and complementary capabilities remain hidden in the physical room.

02 · AVAILABILITY

Who is currently open to meeting someone?

Physical presence does not equal availability. A person nearby could be on a tight deadline, preparing for a call, or taking an open break between tasks.

03 · MUTUAL WELCOME

Would an introduction be mutually welcome?

Without a prior signal of openness, walking up to someone forces a unilateral interruption before knowing whether conversation is welcome.

04 · INITIATION

How to connect without an intrusive approach?

Lacking a low-friction, permissioned way to test mutual interest first, thoughtful professionals usually default to staying quiet.

05 — DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

Five constraints that shape a responsible solution.

Designing for real-world proximity introduces strict human and operational boundaries that directly govern Peopi's product architecture.

01

Privacy

Core Constraint

Nearby discovery must not expose precise, continuous user location or create a sense of physical tracking.

How It Shapes the Solution

Peopi uses approximate relative proximity rings rather than pinpoint street maps, exact coordinates, or background location history.

02

Consent

Core Constraint

Visibility and communication must be intentional, explicit, and reversible at every stage of the experience.

How It Shapes the Solution

Users start invisible by default when opening the app, and direct messaging only unlocks after a recipient accepts an introduction request.

03

Network Density

Core Constraint

The experience requires sufficient relevant, simultaneously available professionals in a local area to avoid empty discovery states.

How It Shapes the Solution

Initial validation focuses on concentrated professional environments and scheduled community windows rather than an unfocused citywide rollout.

04

Trust

Core Constraint

Professional profiles and verification signals must be understandable without implying guaranteed personal safety or endorsement.

How It Shapes the Solution

Profiles present clear professional context and domain tags alongside straightforward reporting, blocking, and connection management tools.

05

User Control

Core Constraint

Participants must be able to end discovery sessions at any moment and manage their visibility without friction.

How It Shapes the Solution

Active sessions run on bounded countdown timers (30, 60, or 120 minutes) with automatic expiration and a one-tap control to go invisible immediately.

06 — PROBLEM STATEMENT & PILOT HYPOTHESIS

Synthesizing the problem and the pilot hypothesis.

Distilling the chapter into a core design question and a single, testable hypothesis for future pilot evaluation.

CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT

“How might we help professionals sharing a physical space discover relevant, mutually welcome introductions without compromising control over their presence and privacy?”

Product Framing: Captures the core tension between unlocking nearby professional serendipity and respecting personal boundaries in shared environments.

PRIMARY PILOT HYPOTHESIS Unvalidated · For Pilot Testing

“In selected Miami professional environments, a time-limited, consent-based discovery experience may help professionals initiate relevant in-person connections that would otherwise be missed.”

Validation Status: This hypothesis is not yet empirically validated. It serves as the primary assumption to be tested in concentrated coworking and event environments.

DECISION ARCHITECTURE

Core product trade-offs & architectural rationale.

Every architectural decision in Peopi resolves a fundamental tension between spontaneous connection and personal privacy. We document the problem, hypothesis, product decision, UX solution, and explicit trade-off for each core mechanic:

DECISION 01 · PRESENCE CONTROL

Time-Limited Visibility Sessions

Problem: Continuous background location tracking causes rapid battery depletion, passive surveillance anxiety, and ghost presence in public spaces.
Hypothesis: Professionals only desire and benefit from visibility during active, dedicated gathering or co-working windows.
Product Decision: Visibility is strictly bound to time-limited sessions (1h, 2h, custom) that auto-expire upon countdown completion.
UX Solution: Explicit session launcher modal with remaining countdown badge on radar and instant "Hide My Presence" kill switch.
Trade-off: Requires conscious, intentional activation; eliminates passive ambient background discovery.
DECISION 02 · PRIVACY BASELINE

Invisible by Default

Problem: Consumer geolocation apps broadcast location immediately upon launch, causing hesitation, privacy violations, and rapid uninstalls.
Hypothesis: High-caliber professionals will only engage if their baseline state is completely unobservable to nearby strangers.
Product Decision: Zero presence is broadcasted upon opening the app; discovery is strictly opt-in requiring two explicit confirmations.
UX Solution: "Invisible Mode" baseline state on radar; unambiguous visual distinction between inactive status and active broadcast.
Trade-off: Introduces cold-start friction; users cannot passively observe peers without first establishing their own session.
DECISION 03 · SPATIAL DISCLOSURE

Approximate Proximity with Spatial Jitter

Problem: Exact GPS coordinates expose users to physical tracking, personal safety risks, and invasive surveillance.
Hypothesis: Knowing someone is within the same venue or general radius (0.5–1 mi) provides full networking context without coordinate exposure.
Product Decision: Proximity is displayed solely as approximate tiered bands ("Nearby · ~300m", "Within 1 mi") with intentional spatial jitter.
UX Solution: Distance bands on discovery cards and radar instead of exact map pins or real-time navigational vectors.
Trade-off: Cannot be used as turn-by-turn indoor navigation; users must coordinate directly to physically locate one another.
DECISION 04 · COMMUNICATION BOUNDARY

Bilateral Mutual Consent (Double Opt-In)

Problem: Open messaging in location-aware apps leads to spam, cold outreach harassment, and swift user departure.
Hypothesis: Direct communication must be earned through verified mutual interest and bilateral relevance.
Product Decision: Zero unsolicited messaging; connection request with context note must be accepted before any chat channel unlocks.
UX Solution: Dedicated Request & Review flow with explicit accept/decline controls and 72-hour automated request expiration.
Trade-off: Introduces asynchronous waiting friction; eliminates instantaneous cold outreach.
DECISION 05 · RELATIONSHIP COMPOUNDING

Persistent Network & Historical Context

Problem: Ephemeral networking tools (AirDrop, Bluetooth beacons) lose connection history once participants leave the venue.
Hypothesis: Professional networking value compounds over time, well beyond the duration of a single physical event.
Product Decision: Accepted connections and conversation threads persist permanently in the user's network directory.
UX Solution: "My Network" directory with searchable profiles, historical context tags, and direct continuing chat channels.
Trade-off: Requires persistent backend data retention, database storage, and offline state synchronization.
DECISION 06 · REAL-WORLD BRIDGING

Immediate Contextual 1-on-1 Chat

Problem: When mutual interest is established in a live room, momentum dies if conversation cannot happen immediately inside the app.
Hypothesis: Prompt, context-rich in-app messaging bridges the gap between digital discovery and real-world conversation.
Product Decision: Automatic creation of an active 1-on-1 thread upon request acceptance, pre-populated with context and intent notes.
UX Solution: Dedicated conversation screen with rich keyboard, presence status, and meetup coordination quick-prompts.
Trade-off: Requires real-time WebSocket messaging infrastructure, push notification pipelines, and state management in MVP.
DECISION 07 · COLD-START DISCIPLINE

Hyper-Focused Tech & Innovation Ecosystem

Problem: Broad horizontal networking platforms suffer from extreme cold-start dilution and low density of relevant peers.
Hypothesis: Concentrating on tech founders, engineers, and product operators in dense innovation hubs creates high matching utility with minimal initial user base.
Product Decision: Scope initial launch, onboarding taxonomy, and vocabulary exclusively to tech, design, and venture professionals.
UX Solution: Role taxonomies, project showcase fields, and curated intent tags tailored to builders and operators.
Trade-off: Deliberately excludes general-purpose social or non-tech consumer networking in the initial release.

DOUBLE OPT-IN LIFECYCLE

Request states.

Clear handling of introduction requests with total recipient privacy and voluntary connection.

Awaiting action

Pending

The recipient receives the introduction note with headline and shared context. Direct chat remains locked.

Sender control: The sender can cancel or withdraw the request at any time prior to recipient response.
Mutual consent

Accepted

1-on-1 direct messaging unlocks immediately. Both members are added to each other’s network.

Continuous access: Messaging remains permanently open even after the proximity session terminates.
Quiet resolution

Declined or canceled

Distinguishes recipient decisions from sender withdrawals to ensure psychological safety:

• Declined by recipient: Quietly closes without awkward notifications or rejection alerts.
• Canceled by sender: Withdrawn before an answer, removing it from pending queues.
SESSION INVARIANT

Discovery ends. Your network stays.

Ending an active discovery session (or timing out) simply turns off live radar visibility. It never removes existing connections or messages. Your network and conversation histories remain permanently accessible in your account.

EDGE CASES & POLICIES

Special situations.

Product principles and edge-case handling for honest and reliable interaction.

Peopi maintains an honest empty state. It never simulates phantom users, fake active avatars, or artificial proximity activity to create artificial engagement. If no active members are in range, Peopi transparently communicates this and suggests checking back during conference breaks or moving to higher-density community areas.
Live proximity discovery requires device location permissions to calculate relative nearby presence. Without location permission, the radar cannot activate. The product team is currently assessing potential fallback alternatives (such as manual venue check-in codes or event hashtags), but no alternative fallback policy has been decided yet.
“Hide My Presence” specifically removes you from a specific connection’s live proximity radar so they do not see you on their map or list during that session. However, it preserves your existing direct chat and connection. This is distinct from “Block”, which permanently severs connection, locks messaging, and prevents any mutual interaction.
