WHAT IS PEOPI? · PRODUCT MANAGEMENT CASE STUDY

Product Overview.

Proximity-based professional networking designed to turn physical co-presence into intentional, high-trust relationships.

Peopi is a mobile product concept designed to turn physical proximity into intentional, high-trust professional relationships. In places where relevant peers share the same room—conferences, coworking hubs, and innovation districts—Peopi replaces awkward cold outreach with a permissioned, consent-first introduction loop.

PRODUCT STRATEGY

Strategic choices grounded in human trust.

Strategic differentiators explained through deliberate product reasoning rather than arbitrary feature checklists.

01

Default Invisible Presence

Strategic Choice

Launching or opening the app never broadcasts availability. Presence remains completely dark until an active session is explicitly initiated.

Product Rationale (Why)

Passive beaconing creates pervasive surveillance anxiety. Making visibility an explicit, active choice lowers adoption friction and protects member trust.

02

Time-Limited Discovery Sessions (30, 60, or 120 minutes)

Strategic Choice

Every discovery session runs on an active countdown timer of 30, 60, or 120 minutes. When the session expires, visibility automatically reverts to dark presence.

Product Rationale (Why)

Professional availability is inherently temporary. Time-boxing prevents "zombie profiles" and ensures that anyone visible is genuinely present and available right now.

03

Approximate Proximity & Privacy Safeguards

Strategic Choice

Relative distance rings (Immediate, Nearby, Walking distance) without live GPS coordinates, continuous tracking, or pinpoint maps.

Product Rationale (Why)

Precise coordinates expose members to unwanted tracking. The design aims to reduce physical tracking, triangulation, and inference risks through approximate presence, aggregation thresholds, and consent-first controls.

04

Mutual Acceptance Before Messaging

Strategic Choice

Direct chat unlocks exclusively after an introductory request with a short note is accepted by the recipient.

Product Rationale (Why)

Open inboxes degrade into unsolicited outreach and sales pitches. A double opt-in handshake protects attention, reduces unsolicited messaging, and ensures both parties enter conversation with mutual intent.

05

Miami-First Pilot Density Hypothesis

Strategic Choice

Proposed initial testing strictly within selected Miami coworking spaces (e.g. Brickell, Wynwood) and scheduled tech gatherings.

Product Rationale (Why)

Proximity network effects fail when liquidity is spread too thin. Concentrating hypothetical pilot testing in dense, tech-forward gathering spaces allows evaluating core loop viability before considering expansion.

MVP SCOPE & TRADE-OFFS

Consequential MVP decisions and their trade-offs.

Every product decision requires deliberate trade-offs. Here is how four critical architectural compromises were evaluated for the initial scope.

DECISION 01 Visibility Model

Time-Limited Sessions vs. Always-On Tracking

Selected MVP Path

Explicit 30, 60, or 120-minute session countdown with automatic expiry back to dark presence.

Accepted Trade-Off

Lower recorded background daily active time; in exchange for intentional user privacy, minimal background battery consumption by avoiding continuous tracking, and confirmed live presence.

DECISION 02 Communication Architecture

Consent-Based Handshake vs. Open Direct Messaging

Selected MVP Path

Mandatory introduction request with optional 200-character note; recipient holds total agency to accept or decline.

Accepted Trade-Off

Slower communication velocity and added interaction friction; in exchange for reducing unsolicited messaging, protecting recipient attention, and encouraging intentional introductions.

DECISION 03 Proximity Display

Closed Proximity Rings vs. Exact Pinpoint Maps

Selected MVP Path

Abstract radar preview with coarse distance zones; no coordinates, pin markers, or continuous background GPS.

Accepted Trade-Off

Users cannot pinpoint a person's exact physical desk or chair; in exchange for reducing triangulation, stalking, and location-inference risks through approximate distance zones and aggregation safeguards.

DECISION 04 Audience Positioning

Startup & Tech Focus vs. Broad Professional Categories

Selected MVP Path

Tailored profile tags, headlines, and intent options optimized specifically for founders, builders, and operators.

Accepted Trade-Off

Constrained initial addressable market (TAM); in exchange for high contextual alignment, shared professional vocabulary, and clearer initial focus.

The complete MVP boundary, non-negotiable invariant rules, and trade-off catalog are preserved in Chapter 01.5.

Product Vision & Strategy.

Make relevant professional connections easier to discover when people share physical spaces.

Peopi explores a focused thesis: co-located professionals connect more effectively when they can signal time-bounded availability, assess professional fit, and connect through mutual consent. This chapter outlines the strategic wedge, competitive positioning, core pillars, and pilot boundaries.

PRODUCT VISION

Long-term ambition and practical product promise.

Distinguishing the broad long-term ambition from the focused MVP specification tested today.

LONG-TERM PRODUCT VISION
“Turn shared physical environments into opportunities for trusted professional connection.”

Over the long term, Peopi envisions an environment where sharing a workspace or conference no longer means working in isolation. Professionals have a trusted way to recognize relevant peers nearby without intrusive tracking.

PRACTICAL PRODUCT PROMISE

“Discover nearby peers, express availability intentionally, and connect through bilateral consent.”

Rather than maintaining another social feed or continuous location tracker, Peopi commits to a narrow loop: opt into a bounded session, assess professional fit, and connect through mutual consent.

Long-Term Horizon Everyday Professional Serendipity

A behavioral norm where co-located professionals activate time-boxed availability during open work blocks.

Current MVP Scope Focused Validation Vehicle

A disciplined prototype testing whether session-based discovery and mutual consent create useful in-person introductions in concentrated settings.

Why the startup and technology community is the initial focus.

Targeting an initial audience with high collaboration needs and frequent physical co-location.

CONTEXT 03

Informal Tech Hubs and Meetups

WEDGE RATIONALE

Acute Need for Complementary Skills

Early-stage builders continually assemble complementary teams, experiencing immediate utility from discovering nearby expertise during active work hours.

PILOT HYPOTHESIS

Testing the local interaction density hypothesis in Miami.

A geographically bounded pilot tests whether concentrated physical overlap produces viable discovery liquidity.

Concentrated Geographic Footprint

Hyperlocal discovery fails if users are dispersed across multiple cities. Bounding the pilot to Miami isolates whether co-located professionals choose to opt in.

Because sessions are time-boxed to 30, 60, or 120 minutes, value requires concurrent sessions in the same venues. The pilot evaluates if natural work rhythms produce this overlap.

HYPOTHESIS 04

Evidence Before Geographic Expansion

Expansion to new cities depends on observing consistent opt-ins, mutual request acceptance, and repeat usage within the initial Miami test environment.

MVP Scope & Product Decisions.

The MVP is an intentionally narrow instrument validating whether co-located professionals will discover each other, mutually consent to connect, and meet in person.

By enforcing explicit session boundaries, approximate spatial halos, and reciprocal visibility, Peopi addresses core proximity failure modes: surveillance anxiety, phantom presence, and unsolicited spam.

CORE PRODUCT LOOP

Five-state interaction progression.

STATE 01

Invisible

Default baseline. Zero background broadcast, complete location privacy.

STATE 02

I'm Open

Conscious opt-in with fixed presets (30, 60, or 120m) and intent tags.

STATE 03

Discover

Reciprocal scanning via approximate halos; aggregate activity when closed if permitted.

STATE 04

Connect

Bilateral request with optional 140-char note; silent dismissals protect dignity.

STATE 05

Chat

Direct messaging unlocked upon mutual acceptance; thread persists permanently.

Gateway Criterion: Any feature that does not directly advance a co-located user through this five-state loop during an active physical window is excluded from the MVP.
PRODUCT DECISION LOG

Architectural decisions, alternatives, and accepted trade-offs.

Architectural choices establishing trust, defensibility, and user privacy:

DECISION A · PRESENCE ARCHITECTURE

Opt-In Visibility Sessions vs. Always-On Background Presence

Problem: Background tracking causes privacy anxiety, battery drain, and stale presence signals.
DECISION

Explicit, user-initiated sessions: Invisible by default; discovery terminates automatically at countdown expiration.

ALTERNATIVE

Passive background geofencing: Continuous tracking with push alerts when contacts are near.

Trade-off: Users must remember to start a session, sacrificing passive serendipity for verified presence and privacy confidence.
DECISION B · TEMPORAL CONTROL

Fixed Preset Durations (30, 60, 120m) vs. Indefinite or Custom Timers

Problem: Open-ended discovery leaves phantom profiles; custom duration pickers cause decision fatigue.
DECISION

Three presets: 30m (coffee), 60m (session), 120m (conference), with one-tap +30m extensions and manual exit.

ALTERNATIVE

Unbounded discovery: Continuous "until turned off" mode or a freeform time picker allowing arbitrary minutes.

Trade-off: Reduced flexibility for atypical blocks; requires user action to extend visibility.
DECISION C · SPATIAL PRIVACY

Approximate Proximity with Soft Halos vs. Exact Live GPS Pins

Problem: Exact GPS coordinates enable stalking and create an unwelcome surveillance atmosphere in professional venues.
DECISION

Approximate halos: Relative proximity indicators and distance buckets without pinpointing coordinates or indoor headings.

ALTERNATIVE

High-precision GPS pins: Indoor navigation markers directing users to an individual's exact desk.

Trade-off: Users must coordinate meeting spots visually or via chat rather than automated desk-level locating.
DECISION D · RECIPROCAL TRANSPARENCY

Aggregated Activity Zones & Honest Empty States vs. One-Sided Lurking

Problem: Inactive browsing creates asymmetric lurking; blank maps during off-hours induce churn.
DECISION

Reciprocal visibility: Browse active profiles only while sharing presence. Closed state displays aggregate activity zones when privacy thresholds permit, or an honest empty state.

ALTERNATIVE

Asymmetric browsing: Browse-only mode for invisible users, or simulated bot profiles during low-density hours.

Trade-off: Users arriving before an event cannot scout attendees until they publish their own presence.
DECISION E · COMMUNICATION ARCHITECTURE

Single Mutual Acceptance Model vs. Multi-Tiered Message Approvals

Problem: Open messaging invites spam; multi-tier approvals stall in-person momentum.
DECISION

Bilateral request: Includes optional 140-char note. Chat unlocks only upon mutual acceptance; declines are silent.

ALTERNATIVE

Multi-tiered messaging: LinkedIn-style InMail credits or open message inboxes.

Trade-off: No pre-acceptance follow-up messages; conversations require mutual acceptance. Connections and chats persist permanently.
DECISION F · COLD-START STRATEGY

Tech & Startup Ecosystem in Miami vs. Broad Horizontal Rollout

Problem: Hyperlocal networks fail when diffusion outpaces local physical density.
DECISION

Constrained vertical pilot: Focused on tech founders, engineers, and investors in candidate Miami hubs (Brickell, Wynwood) as a proposed pilot.

ALTERNATIVE

Horizontal nationwide launch: Open rollout across diverse professional fields and geographic regions simultaneously.

Trade-off: Narrow initial market; unable to serve external demand during pilot evaluation.
DECISION G · IDENTITY & TRUST

Progressive Distinct Verification Signals vs. Mandatory Upfront ID Gate

Problem: Mandatory ID scans cause onboarding drop-off; unverified accounts erode trust.
DECISION

Progressive trust: Signup via Apple, Google, or email, with optional LinkedIn linking and progressive verification signals.

ALTERNATIVE

Upfront identity gating: Mandatory government ID scans and employer verification before app entry.

Trade-off: Relies on community reporting to flag bad actors before full multi-factor verification is completed.

02 · SESSION LIFECYCLE

Bounded presence and relationship continuity.

How discovery sessions manage time, visibility expiration, and network retention.

Preset Durations & Extension

Sessions offer three preset durations: 30, 60 (default), or 120 minutes. Users can apply an optional 30-minute extension before expiry. Custom durations are rejected to prevent continuous presence; capping extensions per session remains an open production policy.

Manual Early Exit

Tapping "Go invisible" ends discovery immediately. Broadcasting ceases, returning to the closed baseline without lingering presence tokens.

Automatic Expiration

When the timer hits zero, visibility terminates automatically. Prototype simulation runs locally; production requires authoritative server-side time-to-live enforcement independent of client clocks.

Network Retention

Discovery termination affects live radar visibility only. Accepted connections and chat histories persist in My Network (Screen 03.1), remaining accessible unless removed or blocked by either user.

CORE INVARIANT

Discovery ends. Your network stays.

Ending a session disables live proximity visibility only without disconnecting accepted peers or clearing chat history. In the prototype, pending requests remain in the recipient queue until accepted, declined, or cancelled by the sender.

03 · PRIVACY & CONSENT JOURNEY

Critical boundaries across the interaction lifecycle.

Explicit safeguards govern presence, proximity resolution, and bilateral relationship controls.

Closed State: Aggregate Activity & Honest Empty States

Peopi displays broad aggregate activity zones or an honest zero state. It never renders individual pins, names, or artificial counts to manufacture engagement.

Active Discovery: Spatial Halos & Initialed Avatars

Active radar displays soft diffusion halos and distance bands without revealing indoor location. Spatial architecture avoids transmitting raw GPS coordinates between peers. Unconnected profiles show initials-based avatars with portraits withheld.

Connection Gate: Double Opt-In Verification

Direct messaging unlocks only after mutual acceptance. Exact GPS coordinates are never revealed before or after connection.

Established Connections: Hide My Presence, Mute, and Block

Peopi provides three distinct boundary controls for connected contacts:
• Hide My Presence: Conceals live radar visibility from a specific connection without notifying them. The connection and chat history remain intact unless removed or blocked.
• Mute: Silences message notifications without altering visibility or connection status.
• Block: Terminates discovery and communication. Post-block chat transcript retention is an open backend policy decision.

Core validation question.

Can time-limited, opt-in proximity discovery produce meaningful professional introductions in a concentrated local community?

Professionals will activate discovery for 30, 60, or 120 minutes given explicit time limits, approximate halos, and one-tap invisibility.

Relevance and Mutual Acceptance

Hypothesis

Profile cards with structured intent notes provide sufficient context to evaluate relevance and accept requests.

Observable Signal

High request acceptance ratio and short response latency.

Hypothesis

Mutual acceptance provides enough safety to bridge into an in-person conversation.

Proposed North Star Metric Primary Indicator

Mutually Accepted Introductions per Active Session Hour

What it measures: Conversion rate of active discovery sessions into mutually accepted digital introductions. In-person meetings are tracked separately.
Numerator: Mutually accepted connection requests initiated during active discovery sessions.
Denominator: Cumulative hours members spent in active discovery sessions.
Why it matters: Evaluates how effectively active presence enables mutual digital introductions without maximizing screen time.

LEADING INDICATORS · DISCOVERY LIQUIDITY & INTENT
  • Session Activation Rate: Active members starting timed sessions weekly.
  • Concurrent Venue Density: Visible profiles per cluster during peak hours (proposed metric).
  • Outbound Request Velocity: Connection requests sent per session hour.
OUTCOME INDICATORS · CONNECTION QUALITY & SAFETY
  • Mutual Acceptance Ratio: Proportion of outbound requests accepted.
  • Coordination Messaging Rate: Matched pairs exchanging messages within 15 minutes.
  • Voluntary Meeting Confirmation: Separate check evaluating physical meeting follow-through.
  • Safety Incident Rate: Sessions resulting in blocks, reports, or privacy mutes.
